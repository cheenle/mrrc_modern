01. Executive Summary

The MRRC Terminal is a handheld operating position for a remote amateur radio: an ESP32-C3 in a FoloToy AI Passport case that carries one TLS session to an mrrc_modern server, plays the receiver’s audio, shows a truthful waterfall, and transmits when the operator holds PTT.

It exists because a browser on a phone is not an operating position. A phone locks its screen, cannot hold a key comfortably, and cannot be operated with gloves. This device does one thing: it behaves like the front panel of a radio that happens to be somewhere else.

What makes it non-obvious

Four decisions shaped every other one, and each came from a measurement that contradicted the intuition:

  • one socket, not four (AD-001) — four TLS sessions do not fit in this chip’s contiguous memory, and rebuilding a TX socket at key-up loses the first syllable;
  • PCM16, not Opus (AD-002) — the decoder cost ~24 KB and ~60% of the core;
  • 16 kHz forever (AD-003) — at 48 kHz the codec captures peaks of 468/32767, so the “quiet microphone” was the sample rate;
  • the key was never the fault (AD-004) — “PTT releases itself” was a congested uplink: the server was sending 30 KB/s down while the device sent 32 KB/s up.

Who it is for

An operator who is away from the shack, holding one hand-held object, wanting to listen, occasionally transmit, and see where the band is. Not for contesting, not for FT8, not for remote desktop.

What “done” looks like

Ten success criteria in chapter 03 §3.4, each with the instrument that would show it broken: continuous audio for a sustained session, a transmission that survives a mobile link, largest never below the TLS handshake floor, a device that can be re-provisioned without a computer, and a field failure diagnosable from one serial line.

Boundaries

The server owns the radio, the session policy, the keyer arbitration and the audio levels it sends. This firmware owns the codec, buffers, tasks, display, provisioning and the device half of the transmit path. The radio’s TOT is the last guaranteed stop; nothing in the firmware can replace it.

Record status

Chapters 00–15 are written; everything here is measured on the unit named in README.md, and every claim carries its instrument, date and scenario (chapter 00 §V1). Where a number is bench-only, the row says implemented rather than field-exercised. Nothing is stubbed: a chapter that is not written is marked pending and cannot be cited.
